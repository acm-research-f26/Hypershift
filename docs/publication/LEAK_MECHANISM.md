# Mechanism decomposition of the back-adjustment term (publication revision)

Descriptive analysis on data already on disk: the audit's unadjusted download (2014-06 to 2024-01-11) and the FB saved labels (factor-neutral 5-day returns, 2019–2023 signals, 462 current S&P 500 stocks). No model is fitted, no backtest is run, and no new period is used. All years were inspected before. Generated by `scripts/pub_leak_mechanism.py`.

**Identity used.** L(t) = L(end) + Σ_{t<e≤end} log(1 − y_e), with y_e = D_e/P_{e−1} recovered from the jumps of L at ex-dates. Detected distribution events: 12989. Negative jumps of L (revisions or rounding; not modelled): 9.

**Counterfactual.** L_div replaces each future pre-ex-date price P_{e−1} with the current price P(t). It keeps the realised future distribution amounts but removes their dependence on the future price path. L_path is the remainder. Both components use post-t information: L_div through future dividend amounts, L_path through future prices.

Mean per-date Spearman correlation: L_div vs L_path -0.123; L vs L_div +0.990.

**Reading rules (fixed before the numbers):**
- If L_path carries a positive association, the future-price channel is empirically present.
- If L_div carries a positive association, future distribution amounts (dividend policy outcomes) are associated with returns.
- Neither component is a causal estimate. Both are ex-post quantities unavailable at t, so any association is look-ahead by construction. Their sizes describe how much predictive content the contamination can carry in this sample.
