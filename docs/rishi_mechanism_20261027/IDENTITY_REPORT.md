# Stable stock preference versus changing information

2026-10-27. Research_Plan Phase B. **Disposition: descriptive evidence supports persistent, concentrated exposure; the learned mechanism remains unresolved.** This report delivers the available identity, stock-P&L, selection, availability and membership analyses. It does not close missing Hpred, changing-score, dated-sector or causal style-control requirements.

## What is established

The verified exported strategy is real under its specified accounting model: the original accounts and all twelve headline paths reproduce. Daily stock profits, fees, quantities, economic marks and cash reconcile. In the highest-Sharpe original-five/20%-target account, Alphabet, NVIDIA and Broadcom contribute 75.64% of total net annual arithmetic return. The ten-seed counterpart's top three contribute 74.15%. These are additive return-contribution fractions, not fractions of Sharpe or proof that any neural component caused the exposure.

Within each annual fitted model, between-stock differences account for 87.65–96.17% of the ten-seed blend's eligible rank-score variation. Adjacent-origin ranks correlate .931–.973 on average per year. This is strong evidence that the strategy repeatedly prefers similar stocks. It does not distinguish indexed neural weights from persistent features or repeatedly favorable changing signals.

SCORE_DECOMPOSITION.csv retains all 36 fold/model/group cells. Its means are descriptive held-out quantities; they were not used as trading controls. STOCK_ATTRIBUTION.csv consolidates 2,966 stock rows over 21 distinct accounts, checks and removes the duplicated unmanaged/leader account, and retains fees, holding days and stage provenance. The source uses economic adjusted-price units and settlement approximations, not certified broker shares.

## The available identity control is Hlabel

Hlabel is the mean observed five-session return for each stable stock ID in the annual training fold. At least 30 training labels are required; sparse IDs receive the median qualifying training-ID mean. This is a cheap past-return preference, not the neural model's learned identity intercept.

At ten seeds/top10:

| Account | Net CAGR | Sharpe |
|---|---:|---:|
| Original blend E |23.92%|.755|
| Parent B |15.36%|.519|
| Dynamic D |15.32%|.530|
| Hlabel alone |14.77%|.480|
| Parent blended with Hlabel |22.71%|.675|

The direct primary E-minus-parent+Hlabel annual arithmetic mean is −.42 pp, with 95% interval [−11.35,+10.10] pp and Holm p .5362. Its uncertainty is much wider than the ±2 pp equivalence margin. Similar point CAGRs do not prove equivalence or establish that identity explains everything. The E-minus-parent contrast also fails the primary family threshold (Holm .0604). No financial-retraining gate has passed.

Per-origin outcomes tell the same limited story: the top10 label-return difference between E and parent+Hlabel is .0059 pp, with interval [−.2035,+.2069] pp. Frictionless observed-label returns are not the traded-account estimand; labels affect scoring only, never portfolio eligibility.

## Persistent preference also carries ordinary risk characteristics

Hlabel ranks correlate positively with as-of beta, volatility and liquidity. Mean beta correlations are .506, .591, .080 and .368 across 2022–2025; volatility correlations are .386, .303, .066 and .208. Momentum is inconsistent across years. This makes risk/style preference a concrete competing explanation for a past-winner control's returns. These descriptive cross-sectional correlations do not establish that the neural model learned those same preferences or that its incremental returns survive a style-matched account.

All feature windows end at the origin close, with explicit history/finite-price requirements. Reviewed NDX inputs contain no dated sector classification; current sector tags were not substituted. Dated-sector attribution and a causal style-matched performance comparison remain open. The first-publication availability of historical membership/provider bars is not independently certified.

## Availability and entry/exit sensitivities

A separately frozen common screen keeps original scores and permits only original-eligible names with finite raw reversal/momentum and at least 30 annual training labels. It retains 81–90 names per origin, without consulting test labels or recomputing neural peers. Nineteen matched common-screen accounts do not rescue the increment: top10 E-minus-parent+Hlabel annual mean is −1.06 pp [−11.53,+9.30] pp; all fixed sensitivities include zero. This sensitivity does not replace the original primary universe.

Membership cohorts partition existing recorded P&L into outside-snapshot, recent current member (1–126 sessions) and established member. All ten-seed/top5/full return contribution comes from established members. In top10, recent members contribute −.087 pp/year and outside-snapshot holdings +.061 pp/year versus +26.189 pp from established members. These are source-membership descriptions, not IPO age, an exclusion strategy or an unseen-ID test. End-of-day held-security-day counts and contributions are separately labeled.

## What remains necessary

Hpred and changing-score controls require the original frozen model's training predictions. They cannot be replaced by test-period means, Hlabel or a neural refit. Strict numerical checkpoint reproduction still fails locally, despite exact input reconstruction and stable repeat inference. Therefore no surrogate or trained feature/relation intervention has been promoted as a resolved explanation.

The current working hypothesis is that stable upper-tail preferences and exposure to a few historical winners materially produce the account path, with the risk rule changing its drawdown/Sharpe. Which learned channels or relations generate useful changing information remains **DEFER**. A different architecture, a larger RankIC or synthetic learning capacity alone cannot settle that question.
