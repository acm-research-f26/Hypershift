# Rishi model investigation: meeting brief

**October 29, 2026 — research update, not a trading recommendation**

## The answer in one minute

Rishi's results are reproducible as historical simulations under the documented inputs and execution assumptions. The strongest explanation supported so far is a combination of **persistent stock preferences, a rank-blending rule that changes the basket, and risk controls that reduce exposure and drawdowns**. Some of those preferences resemble liquidity and market-beta exposure. We have **not established which information the neural network learned, whether dynamic hypergraph relationships add unique predictive value, or whether the results will hold in future trading**.

The validation-preference tests are particularly informative: a fixed ranking derived from earlier validation forecasts performs similarly at the selected five-issuer risk-managed point estimate, but underperforms at the ten-stock full-exposure point estimate. Both paired intervals are wide. Validation was used to select checkpoints, so this is a descriptive control rather than the planned training-prediction control. It cannot prove either equivalence or a causal explanation.

## What creates the historical results?

1. **Selection and rank blending.** Each parent and dynamic model's seed forecasts are ranked across eligible stocks; seed ranks are averaged within family, then parent and dynamic ranks are blended equally. This creates a new basket. In the ten-seed top-ten historical comparison, the blend has **23.92% CAGR / 0.755 Sharpe**, versus **15.36% / 0.530** for an executable 50:50 capital split of the standalone books. This difference also reflects different positions, turnover, cash and costs; it is not a clean causal estimate of neural complementarity. [Blend evidence](BLEND_BOUNDARY_REPORT.md).
2. **Stable preferences.** Ten-seed validation-to-test stock mean-rank correlations are **0.966–0.995** across four folds. A ranking frozen from validation shares **40%, 81%, 78%, 73%** of target weight with the changing five-issuer targets by year. These statistics support persistence; they do not show whether it comes from indexed parameters, persistent inputs or other mechanisms. Test-period averages are retrospective; validation selected checkpoints. [Rank persistence](execution_batch46_validation_rank/REPORT.md); [decision-level overlap](execution_batch48_validation_fixed/REPORT.md).
3. **Risk management.** For the ten-seed issuer-five book, the 20% basket-volatility rule changes the historical point result from **33.37% CAGR / 0.925 Sharpe / -40.94% max drawdown** at full exposure to **26.81% / 1.112 / -21.69%**. It adjusts exposure and leaves unused capital in zero-interest cash; it is not a hard drawdown stop. Similar overlays helped several simpler controls, and paired Sharpe intervals were wide. [Risk attribution](RISK_ATTRIBUTION_REPORT.md).
4. **A later hedge result is not model timing.** The source's selected always-on QQQ-short control reports ten-seed **1.325 Sharpe / 24.83% CAGR**. The tested raw model timing signals never activated, and the calibrated signals activated only in 2023. Local reconstruction checked the signals and 12 account implementations; the original hedge daily-path archive was not published in this checkout. The hedge assumes borrow and execution conditions and its paired interval includes zero. [Hedge assessment](HEDGE_STUDY_ASSESSMENT_20261029.md).

## What was verified—and what was not

- **Verified:** 1,184 originally published artifact hashes, 18 original account paths, and the later matched controls documented in the [completion audit](COMPLETION_AUDIT.md). The newly published raw supplement's 2,660 file hashes pass; the entire two extended-hours feature cubes reproduce from 1,288 raw chunks under the source formulas, and the daily panels reconstruct from the saved sources and recorded assumptions. This is reproducibility, not independent proof of vendor history.
- **Not verified:** historical first-publication time, revisions and five-minute data delivery; authoritative point-in-time membership and price identity; real broker fills, auction capacity, short locates and borrowing costs. The monthly membership rule differs from the pinned source's daily-effective list at **31 of 201 test decisions**; this is not an account-impact estimate. [Timing](FEATURE_TIMING_RECHECK_20261029.md); [membership](MEMBERSHIP_STALENESS_20261029.md).
- **Checkpoint gate remains open:** local inference fails the frozen **1e-6 percentage-point** reproduction tolerance for 31 of 80 selected checkpoints on at least one split: 30 parent and one dynamic. In the fixed unmodified-inference diagnostic, top-five and top-ten weights did not change, although some full eligible rankings did; that does not establish parity for interventions or training predictions. A local near-boundary numerical amplification was identified, but it does not reproduce the original machine. Saved-export replay and checkpoint regeneration are different claims. The user asked to set the original-machine trace aside for now; that does not waive the gate. [All-checkpoint diagnostic](execution_batch18_cuda_rank/REPORT.md); [numerical diagnosis](execution_batch41_logmap_precision/REPORT.md).
- **Not yet established:** training-prediction Hpred and changing-score controls, trained-feature and peer interventions, and a prospective confirmation. Financial retraining is conditional on the predeclared evidence and compute gates; the primary increment tests have not met them. The protected S&P window and prospective activation remain separate decisions.

## Suggested meeting decisions

**Questions to put to the group:**

- Are we aligned that the current evidence supports a promising historical portfolio, but does not yet identify a unique learned signal?
- Which would change our decision most: resolving checkpoint reproduction to test trained components, or prioritizing operational data and execution readiness?
- What minimum economic improvement and fixed observation period would justify the next prospective test, given the current power estimate?
- Should the continuous hedge be evaluated as a separate risk-control candidate, including realistic borrow and execution assumptions?

1. **Agree on the present verdict:** promising historical portfolio construction and reproducible accounting, with stable preferences and cost sensitivity; the unique learned signal remains unresolved.
2. **Choose the next discriminating research question**, rather than another historical winner: whether the trained dynamic branch adds changing information beyond the same model's training-period preference and matched simple controls. This requires resolving the numerical gate before the planned Hpred and checkpoint interventions. Do not infer equivalence from wide intervals.
3. **Decide whether to pursue a separately frozen operational candidate and prospective validation** after data-timing, identity, execution and power requirements are addressed. The two-year planning simulation had only **2.32% power** to reject both primary nulls for a +2 pp/year increment under its assumptions; a non-significant short prospective period would be weak evidence either way. [Power report](execution_batch7_power/REPORT.md).
4. **Keep the hedge decision separate:** the high-Sharpe continuous hedge is a searched risk-control observation, not evidence that model forecasts time the market. Actual borrow, costs and exposure require independent operational assessment.
