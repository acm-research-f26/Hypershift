# Sparse industry-matched random-name controls

Protocol ../INDUSTRY_SPARSE_RANDOM_PROTOCOL.md (86aa923) and implementation 3217db6 were fixed before scoring. For each original ten-seed top10 decision, each of 20 predeclared controls preserves the number of positive positions and exact positive-weight multiset **inside every available FF12 industry and the unclassified bucket**, then assigns those weights to names using fixed random priorities within each fold. The same 20 priority sets are used for E and E_Hlabel. Thus this comparison removes the earlier control's roughly 58-versus-10 position-count difference at target formation, while changing which stocks receive the weights. It does not force the original names, turnover, actual fills, beta or realized risk to match.

The control mean is an arithmetic average of the 20 daily return paths, each generated by a separately funded $1m account; it is an algebraic diagnostic, **not** the return of an executable pooled $1m account. Individual E controls' net CAGR ranges from -1.71% to 24.41% and Sharpe from -0.037 to 0.858; E's own CAGR and Sharpe are 23.92% and 0.755. E's arithmetic mean exceeds each control's arithmetic mean, but individual controls can exceed its CAGR or Sharpe: those are different quantities. The pre-fixed control 0 reports 8.89% CAGR and 0.336 Sharpe versus E's 23.92% and 0.755. Control outcomes vary because the chosen names matter; no control was selected as a winner. All individual metrics and paths are retained.

For comparison, the E_Hlabel controls range from 0.26% to 25.18% CAGR and 0.054 to 0.886 Sharpe; its original account reports 22.71% and 0.675. Both model accounts historically exceed their respective **average** sparse controls in arithmetic mean, so the result does not isolate a contribution unique to the dynamic model. Across E controls, annual turnover averages 13.63× versus 24.91× for E. The common industry target composition and position-size pattern are matched, but within-industry selection, name concentration, risk, cost and turnover remain different; a randomized-name control cannot tell whether model gains come from persistent historical winners, changing signals, omitted factors or chance.

The intervals use the fixed paired market-time bootstrap, and block-5 and block-63 sensitivity intervals are in PAIRED_AVERAGES.csv. They condition on these 20 fixed priority sets and saved models; they do not measure uncertainty across new random-control designs, model refits or the previous research search. The controls inherit each model's entire changing industry-budget, position-count and tie-weight schedule, so they are not standalone industry-only strategies. The intervals are pointwise exploratory estimates on repeatedly inspected NDX 2022–2025 history, not search-adjusted confirmation; the 20 controls are not independent market histories. This test does not alter the original E-versus-parent/Hlabel gate or justify conditional financial retraining. Classification snapshots, issuer identity, membership vintage and reconstructed economic execution remain subject to prior limitations. The unclassified bucket is not a sector.

Both original accounts replayed the saved paths before any control was scored, with daily error below 1e-16 and normalized NAV error below 4.66e-16. Independent conceptual review found no substantive implementation issue and prompted the conditional-uncertainty and inherited-schedule clarifications above. An independent numerical audit reconstructed all 88,400 group constraints, all 40 control weight arrays from their fixed priorities, every control's metrics/NAV/ledger summaries, and six paired mean intervals; maximum group mass error was 2.22e-16. Nine focused tests passed.
