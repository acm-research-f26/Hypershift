# Does a fixed validation preference reproduce the account?

For the ten-seed issuer-five book with the 20% volatility target, the existing model account and a fixed validation-preference account have similar **historical point results**: **26.81% versus 26.71% CAGR**, and **1.112 versus 1.080 Sharpe**, respectively. The paired annual arithmetic net-mean difference (model minus fixed) is **-0.05 percentage points**, with a 21-session stationary-bootstrap 95% interval of **[-6.77, +6.99] pp**. The interval is much wider than the declared ±2 pp economic margin. It establishes neither equivalence nor superiority.

Under full exposure, the ten-seed model reports **33.37% CAGR / 0.925 Sharpe**, versus **27.11% / 0.788** for fixed validation preference. The paired annual mean difference is **+4.92 pp [-7.14, +18.48]**, again wide. The comparison depends on exposure policy: a risk-managed account uses each basket's own trailing volatility estimate. Fixed-preference turnover is much lower: **4.56 versus 22.68 times annually** for the risk-managed ten-seed books. Different exposures, turnover and fees are part of the account comparison, not a pure estimate of changing neural information.

All six group/policy mean intervals include zero. Original-five and new-five results, all 12 accounts, four calendar years, and 18 block-size sensitivity rows are in the accompanying tables. Sharpe and CAGR intervals are pointwise; the independently checked mean intervals are not adjusted for multiple comparisons. This post-outcome follow-up uses repeatedly inspected history and overlapping seed groups. Validation selected the original checkpoints, and its saved forecasts are **not training Hpred**. Similar point results do not establish that identity explains the model, that fixed preferences generalize, or that the model learned no useful changing information.
