# Do stock preferences persist from validation into the next test year?

For the ten-seed blend, the Spearman correlation between each stock's mean validation percentile rank and its mean test-year rank is **0.974, 0.969, 0.966 and 0.995** for the 2022–2025 test folds, respectively. The matched samples contain 94, 94, 94 and 97 stock IDs. Median absolute changes in mean percentile rank are 0.011, 0.013, 0.014 and 0.008 on a 0–1 scale. These are cross-sectional summaries of within-fold averages, not predictive return statistics.

This is additional evidence that the saved models' relative stock preferences persist across the validation/test boundary. It does **not** show that an identity parameter causes the persistence: stable inputs, model parameters and related market histories can all contribute. The test-year averages are descriptive and cannot be used as an ex-ante trading signal. Validation was also used for checkpoint selection, so this is not an independent holdout, and its mean forecasts are not the training-prediction Hpred control. High cross-sectional correlation says nothing by itself about the value of changing upper-tail information or future alpha.

The [fixed protocol](../VALIDATION_STABLE_RANK_PROTOCOL_20261029.md) was committed as `24a205a` before these calculations. All 36 predetermined fold/group/family cells are in `CELLS.csv`; 80 distinct saved forecast files were hashed. A separate pandas-based reconstruction reproduced all 36 correlations and median differences to a maximum absolute error of 3.68e-16. Each split's per-seed eligible ranks are computed separately before family averaging; no test labels, new neural inference, fitting or account scoring were used. Groups overlap and share the same market history. The strict checkpoint gate, primary financial decisions and prospective restrictions remain unchanged.
