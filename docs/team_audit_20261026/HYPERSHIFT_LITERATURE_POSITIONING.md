# Literature positioning and verification

**Research gap:** can a fixed equity-ranking ensemble deliver incremental net tail selection after accounting for seed choice, persistent stock exposure, data identity and trading costs? A positive result would support that estimator in that setting. Dynamic-hypergraph causality requires additional matched architecture controls.

## Why these distinctions matter

THINK/STHAN predefined relations, learned SPF membership, StockMixer's stock-indexed matrices, and PRISM's latent factor codebook are not interchangeable “dynamic graph models.” Removing one relational component while changing temporal capacity, features or training budget does not isolate a mechanism. A valid next architecture study must keep these components fixed and test exactly one change with no-graph/static/random controls.

HRCF's centering can be written as `xbar_i=x_i-mean_j(x_j)` in the relevant tangent space. Its radial regularizer is `(mean_i ||xbar_i||²)^(-1/2)`. The former relocates the centroid; the latter discourages collapse toward it. Their separate effects on conditioning and tail portfolio choices should not be combined under one “alignment” label.

## Positioning the proposed contribution

The economic ensemble literature already exists; dynamic hypergraphs and hyperbolic regularization are not novel by themselves. HyperShift should not claim invention of rank blending, frozen seeds, buffering, PIT evaluation or selection-adjusted statistics.

A defensible **positive** contribution would combine a reproducible prediction/ledger contract with genuinely new-date paired gains against the parent, causal identity substitute, simple reversal and matched-universe benchmark. Show whether value comes from time-varying top-tail selections, survives costs and is not solely beta/sector/momentum exposure. A further claim that learned incidence causes the gain requires the exact matched static/no-graph/placebo experiment.

A defensible **negative** contribution would demonstrate a mechanism: e.g., stock-ID mixing creates persistent selected-winner exposure; nonlinear seed aggregation produces unstable tail portfolios; a supposedly relational advantage is matched by random pooling; or gross information decays before feasible execution. Include synthetic recovery gates, full trial registry, failure cases, all seeds and independently replayable outputs. “We tried many models and none was significant” is insufficient.

For **conditional** claims, evaluate the interaction and the complete calendar strategy under a causal frozen state, not only attractive within-state Sharpe. Power and sample-independence must be demonstrated. The present audit supplies no confirmed conditional result.
