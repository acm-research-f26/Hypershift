## A. Sector assignment timing

**Implementation.** `pit_sp500.sector_proxy` assigns each entity the GICS sector whose leave-one-out survivor basket correlates most with the entity's first 126 *observed* daily returns (from 2016-01-04 or listing). Eligibility starts after 64 consecutive observed sessions. So between eligibility and the 126th return, the sector used at a signal close depends on up to 62 later returns: **look-ahead**.

**Size of the effect.** A causal variant using only the first 63 returns (all before the first possible eligible day) changes the sector of 155 of 678 entities; agreement with current GICS on survivors is 0.637 (63 returns) vs 0.728 (126). 
**Current-GICS dependence.** The reference baskets are built from *survivors* (2026 constituents) labelled with *current* GICS. Basket returns are contemporaneous (no timing leak), but the labels are a 2026 classification and only survivors define the baskets: a historical-classification limitation, not an information-timing leak.

**Disposition.** The look-ahead touches a small set of signals (above) and the stopped style book's decision (net Sharpe +0.11, with a GICS-sector sensitivity of −0.17) cannot plausibly move to the 0.60 continuation threshold through it; no rerun. **Pipeline fix:** the panel now also stores `sectors_causal` (63-return rule). The next strategy uses **Fama–French 12 industries from EDGAR SIC codes** instead, which removes both the return-based proxy and the current-GICS dependence (SIC is the registrant's latest code: still not a dated history).

## B. Opening-price accounting

**Construction** (`pit_sp500.price_index`, test `test_price_index_open_uses_no_same_day_close`). The close index cumulates the cleaned daily returns; the open is `index_t × raw_open_t / raw_close_t`, which equals `index_(t−1) × adjustment_t × raw_open_t / raw_close_(t−1)`: it uses the previous close, the day's raw open and the ex-date adjustment factor, **never the same day's close** (the test changes the day's close and the open is unchanged). On an ex-dividend or split day the overnight return includes the adjustment, which is the economic return of a holder. On a neutralised day open = close = previous close.

- Rebuilding the stored panel with the tested function: max relative difference close 0.0e+00, open 0.0e+00.
- Engine fills: `run_pit` trades at `open_t` after drifting weights with close(t−1)→open(t); P&L from open(t)→close(t) accrues to the new weights. Signals use closes up to t−1 only (`test_open_execution_no_lookahead`).
- Neutralised stock-days: 18 in total, 12 on member days (the 12 listed in `PIT_DATA.md`) and 6 on non-member days (CNX 2017-11-29, FTR 2020-03-23, DD 2019-06-03, CHK 2020-04-15, EQT 2018-11-13, MAC 2020-04-21).
- Arm P held a nonzero weight on 6 of them, all hedge-basket sized (max |weight| 0.0002): EQT 2018-11-13 (+0.0002), RTX|UTX 2020-04-03 (-0.0001), ARNC 2020-04-01 (-0.0000), AIV 2020-11-03 (+0.0001), AIV 2020-12-15 (+0.0000), IR 2020-03-02 (+0.0000). No long or short leg position sat on a neutralised day, so the zeroed returns cannot distort the result.

**Treatment by case:**
- *Cash merger*: the target trades until the deal closes near the deal price, so liquidation at the last close captures the economic exit (minus any final-day spread).
- *Stock merger*: the last close reflects exchange ratio × acquirer price; liquidation to cash at that close approximates selling the received shares at the next open (overnight acquirer move ignored).
- *Failure*: last listed close; post-delisting OTC value absent (sensitivity −100% / −30%).
- *Leaving the index while listed*: exited at the next weekly rebalance (`run_pit`), never kept as a stub.
- *Halts / missing bars while a member*: 2887 member-days in 28 entities; longest runs: WYND from 2016-01-04 (606 days), APTV from 2016-01-04 (474 days), BHGE|BKR from 2016-01-04 (378 days), AABA from 2016-01-04 (367 days), IR from 2016-01-04 (342 days), FTI from 2016-01-04 (261 days). A held name with no bar keeps its weight at zero return until its bars resume or it is liquidated as delisted; arm P had 49 held stock-days without a bar.

**Ticker identity vs company identity.** An entity is one Alpaca company series (`asof` = last day of each fja05680 membership interval), merged across intervals whose daily returns are identical; its label lists the historical tickers (e.g. `GEN|NLOK|SYMC`, `CBS|PARA|VIAC`). A reused ticker (DOW = Dow Chemical until 2017 and Dow Inc from 2019) maps to different entities through different `asof` dates. Alpaca's `asof` history can splice predecessor companies around mergers (pre-membership bars of DOW, DD, ARNC); these bars only feed features before membership. **Identity break found:** FBHS (Fortune Brands Home & Security) ends 2022-12-14 although the company continued as FBIN after the MasterBrand spin-off; the `asof` series did not follow the rename, so the engine treated it as a delisting (arm P weight −0.0001, negligible). The general check (a liquidated entity whose CIK keeps filing after its last bar) is run in `CIK_MAP.md`. **For SEC data the identity key is the CIK**, mapped per entity and verified separately (`docs/strategy_lazyprices_20261023/CIK_MAP.md`).

**Remaining limitations.** No post-delisting returns; Alpaca corporate-action records are unreliable for spin-offs (handled by A1); sector/industry codes are current, not dated; membership is the Wikipedia-derived fja05680 list; no prices before 2016.
